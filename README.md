# 24 HR Towing

You run Brandt Towing & Recovery in Esker, Wisconsin. The county is under a tow ban. When Arlene calls, take the heavy wrecker out and find the car by its road and landmarks. Some nights three cold lights keep pace across a field. When they come close, the wind quits.

## Your first call

Start the engine. Tap **RADIO** or press **R** on desktop, then choose **Take contract**. Arlene starts you with a nearby ditch pull. Listen for the road, the landmark and the side the car went off. Your first call might take you past St. Hedwig’s, the co-op or the ginseng frames.

On ordinary pulls and hazard tows, look for a red folding triangle in the snow beside your pickup. If two cars look alike, ask Arlene for directions. She’ll read back the vehicle and plate from your slip.

The dash compass is visible in both driving views. If you lose your way or forget the next step, pause and open **Directions & next action**. In the cab, the truck log gives the next turn from your current road. At the scene, it shows the recovery procedure and what you can do where you’re standing. You can also tap **RADIO → Ask for directions** or press **T** to hear Arlene’s talk-in. Past the edge of her map, use the plat, fire numbers and your own pencil marks.

The green fire plates face both ways along the road. Pull up close, or aim the fitted pillar spot at a plate farther down the shoulder. When the number comes into view, press **G** or tap **CHECK PLAT** to find its owner and road. The plat’s **Fire numbers** index is also available while stopped. On one road, 132 numbers is half a mile. Numbers rise north or west. Roman Duda is checking the pipes at his drying shed beside the ginseng frames north of Esker. Pull into the loading lane and ask him about County K.

Pull over within cable reach and stop before getting out. Stand near the casualty to place a flare. The controls are on the **driver’s side toward the back of the wrecker**. Take the cable there, carry it to the bumper that faces the wrecker, and return to the panel. Hold the winch control and watch the needle. Let off when it climbs toward red. A straight pull is easier than a broadside one.

Ditch customers walk back to their cars and drive away after the pull. Your slip stays on the clipboard. Get back in the cab and answer the radio to go straight to the next call. For a hazard tow, bring the axle up to the underlift and strap it. In the cab, look in a side mirror for the amber ends of the light bar on the towed car’s roof. The loaded truck needs more braking room.

Amber lights mark your drop on every haul. Inside the yard gate, ordinary tows go right to **STORAGE**. For the surveyor’s pickup, bear left toward the office and back the pickup into **EVIDENCE HOLD**. Stop and unhook at the wrecker’s left rear panel. Pull the wrecker clear, then lock the corner chain from the yard side.

When you’re back at the yard, walk to the fax under the **OFFICE / FAX** sign and tap **USE** or press **E**. One transmission files every finished slip in order. Each job gets its own receipt in the fax tray.

Confirming restarts unfinished fieldwork at the original scene, including any car on the hook. Completed deliveries, slips and case notes stay on file. The county carries any unpaid tow charge until your checks come in.

## The county rotation

Arlene draws ditch pulls and checked hazards from seven sites at first. After eight ordinary jobs, she can also send you across the bridge to River Lanes, out to the dairy barn on County FF, or east to the Tamarack county offices. Old slips and county faxes stay on the clipboard.

Open the **Shop** tab to check your account. A new operator starts with $35. Filing a slip adds its pay to **Lifetime earned** and **Pending payment**. Every third slip clears the batch for spending. Buying diesel or parts comes out of **Available**.

Park in the yard and walk to the office fax to buy diesel or fit equipment. You can also park beside either pump at Esker Fuel & Food and use it on foot. An empty-to-full tank costs $48. The shop quotes the top-up, including a partial fill if cash is tight. If you run dry, stop and request a fuel can on the cab radio. The county plow brings a fifth of a tank for **$19.20**, double the pump price. Its 18-minute radio ETA takes about 18 seconds of play. If you're short, the balance goes on an IOU and comes out of future county payments.

A **$175 snatch block** lets you rig a double line for loaded pickups. Once it’s fitted, Arlene can offer county utility trucks on rotation. Heavy recoveries pay an extra $36. **$95 tire chains** improve grip and braking and help the wrecker through deep snow. The office also services a worn winch for $24.

At Esker Fuel & Food, walk through the glass doors to Mai’s counter. A 75¢ coffee warms your hands for two minutes of outdoor work. The shop and Brandt’s heated bay also warm you up. From the full-size plat, choose **Use pencil** to mark a vehicle, lights, or a place to revisit. Choose **Eraser** to remove a mark.

The **Yard fittings** page sells a $145 metered pump for the bulk tank by storage. Park beside it, walk over, and hold the nozzle (or Space) to buy as much diesel as you want. A $39 coffeemaker puts a fresh pot on the bench beside the office. The $245 west fence extension adds twelve numbered spaces behind the five front stalls. Walk up to a stored vehicle to read its windshield tag and original pickup details. The green register beside the front row keeps cards for every impound, including vehicles at county overflow.

The yard catalog’s **Cab & rig** page sells $55 heated mirrors and a $115 driving bar with a pillar spot. To clear stock glass by hand, stand beside the truck, look up at a frosted mirror and use it. With the lamp kit fitted, tap the spot switch or press **L** and look toward the shoulder. The beam follows your view. After meeting Kowalski on Unit 38, you can fit $65 scanner crystals and listen to the sheriff or county garage. Open **Radio → Receivers** to tune the scanner. Highway traffic comes through on the stock set. Each transmission stays in the scanner’s three-call log until another takes its place.

Choose **AM / FM** on the receiver to turn on the cab stereo. **760 AM** carries the Esker farm report and *The Forty Acre Polka*. **99.7 FM** plays *Sunday at River Lanes* and the Tamarack community calendar. The county radio cuts in over either program. Reception gets weaker west of the Tamarack tower. The stereo starts off and remembers the preset you leave it on.

When dispatch sends you to the car up in the Swamp Road tamaracks, park the wrecker’s rear toward it and leave open snow for the landing. Deploy the boom at the side panel, walk to the orange sling with the snatch block, then return to the levers. Push the stick up to lift clear of the branches, right to swing over the verge, and down to land. Use W, D, and S on a keyboard. Releasing the stick holds the load. Once the tires are down, use the winch and wheel-lift for the trip to storage.

With a snatch block fitted, Arlene can send you to Duane’s stuck plow. Go around the buried blade and take the rear recovery eye. Later on rotation, a stranded squad car can earn a $24 quick-clearance bonus. The two-minute allowance starts at flare placement. Take as long as you need to earn the regular fee.

Once you’ve filed a heavy recovery, the yard can fit a **$340 auxiliary winch** alongside the snatch block. When a tractor-trailer jackknifes on US 59, park ahead of its nose. Take the block to the tractor’s front eye, bring the second line to the trailer tandem, then operate both drums at the side panel. Once the trailer is straight, unpin and stow the check line. Pull the tractor onto the wheel lift and tow the whole combination between the four amber posts at River Lanes. The receiver signs on the apron. You can take the next call and fax the freight slip when you’re back at the yard.

Brzezinski’s bulk milk truck can lose its driveline at either farm lane. After setting the flare, read the gallons and seal number on the rear pump cabinet. Use the snatch block on the front eye. Watch the **Tank load** indicator as the milk shifts under the pull. Brake early and ease into corners on the haul. Bring the truck to the lighted milk house on Hendricks and let the tank settle before you lower the lift. The dairy signs for the sealed load there.

The first two jobs give you time to learn the wrecker. If something catches your eye on the road, keep it in view for a moment. For a closer look at something left behind, park, walk over and press **E** or tap **USE**. In the cab, choose **RADIO → Report a sighting**, then pick what you saw. Arlene will log it and tell you what to do. Your current slip stays open. If a vehicle needs a tow and your rig has the equipment, the sheriff can authorize its recovery on a later rotation call.

For a passenger call, park with room at the right cab door, step down, and offer the driver a ride. Wait for them to climb in. Depending on the call, take them to Mai at Esker Fuel & Food or the night entrance at Tamarack Memorial. The blue H is on Highway 64 just east of County FF. Pull into the forecourt or the hospital drop-off, stop, and tap **LET OUT** or press **E**. You can answer the next contract while your rider walks indoors. Check the fax after your fifth ordinary job for the first lead in the county survey case. Your case notes stay in the plat book until you’re ready to call them in. **Keep me on rotation** accepts another paying job and leaves the case waiting.

Choose **Track unpaid stop** on the favor’s fax or on the cab radio. You can make it your next stop even with a paying slip open. Arlene will give you turns, and **Directions & next action** in the truck log tells you what to do on arrival. Look for the red triangle between two amber lamps at a marked stop. You can switch back to the tow at any time. If a lead sits for several calls or a long drive, Arlene gets more specific about where to look. When the favor leads to a paid county assignment, take that call on the radio.

The eight-part case is paced for roughly three hours. Taking each lead when it comes up means filing 48 ordinary jobs and eight case slips. Five to eight fresh ordinary slips bring the next lead after you file each case report. Time on the road, at the winch and out exploring will change how long your shift lasts. The rotation keeps going after the last case slip.

## Build

Use Godot 4.7.2 and matching export templates. Install Git LFS before cloning, or run `git lfs pull` in an existing checkout. Open `project.godot` and run the project, or launch `godot --path .`.

```sh
python3 tools/run_tests.py --godot godot
mkdir -p build
godot --headless --export-release Linux build/24HRTowing.x86_64
```

For a local Android build, configure the Android SDK and JDK 17 in Godot, then run `godot --headless --export-debug Android build/24HRTowing-Android-debug.apk`. The project also includes Linux, Windows and macOS export presets.

## Releases

GitHub Actions tests each pushed revision. By default, each passing revision gets a signed Android ARM APK, Linux and Windows x86-64 packages, a universal ad-hoc-signed macOS app, and SHA-256 checksums. The workflow checks that each desktop package launches on its own operating system. Set the GitHub Actions repository variable `ENABLE_DESKTOP_BUILDS` to `false` for Android-only releases.

Android version codes increase with the workflow run and commit position. When setting up a separate release repository, configure the encrypted Actions secrets `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_BASE64`, `ANDROID_KEY_ALIAS` and `ANDROID_KEY_PASSWORD` for signing.

## Assets

The game uses the bundled OFL copies of Overpass, IBM Plex Mono, Caveat, Architects Daughter, Public Sans and DSEG Classic. Licenses ship in `assets/ui/fonts/`. The material textures, touch-control artwork, evergreen sprite and Brandt sign were made with ImageGen. The title camera moves around the playable wrecker in the live county scene. The launcher icon is a still taken in Godot. The wrecker, vehicles, objects and township are built in Godot. The dash and gauges use original instrument artwork.

Dialogue appears as speaker-tagged subtitles with short electronic pulses synthesized in Godot. Cab settings also offer spoken dialogue using an installed English system voice. The sound effects are local audio files. To generate missing effects, run `python3 tools/generate_audio.py` with an ElevenLabs API key in the environment or a private `~/.config/24hrtowing/elevenlabs.key` file. The tool also recognizes the existing Home Twenty key location. ElevenLabs generation uses API credits. The reverse alarm and the rattle of loose street signs are synthesized locally and encoded with FFmpeg. The installed game plays offline.

The two station tunes are original arrangements recorded with FluidSynth and S. Christian Collins’s GeneralUser GS 2.0.3 instruments. The GeneralUser GS License v2.0 permits use in musical recordings. [GeneralUser GS](https://www.schristiancollins.com/generaluser.php) · [Instrument license](https://github.com/mrbumpy409/GeneralUser-GS/blob/main/documentation/LICENSE.txt)

The AM and FM announcers were rendered with Piper using Bryce Beattie’s US English **ljspeech-medium** and **bryce-medium** voices, respectively. The Piper voice repository lists MIT terms, and both model cards identify their recording datasets as public domain. The station audio ships in the game. The original scores, scripts and render settings are in `tools/generate_broadcasts.py`.
